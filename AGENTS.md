## Motion and access

Transform/opacity and SVG stroke draws only. For interaction-driven layout changes such as widening picker cards, use FLIP or same-document View Transitions with an instant fallback. Looping scenes keep their keys on a 12 s cycle that opens and closes on the static artwork: the Why Augur scenes (`tenets-iso.ts`), the hero flock (`hero-flock.ts`) and the use-case cards (`useCaseTimeline.loop`). v1's scenes shared with Figma (tenets `250:717`, hero `382:848`, the REP rows) left the website with v1 on 29 Oct 2026. SVG animates `transform`, never the individual `translate`/`scale` properties, which re-layerize every frame in Chromium, and avoids per-frame paint such as `stroke-dashoffset`. The hero's headline and support line paint at once and only rise 8 px, because a fade held back the Largest Contentful Paint (`heroTimeline`; scroll-motion brief, 29 Oct 2026). The roadmap Now stop keeps the one slow CSS pulse, which rests while the roadmap is off screen. Other authored scenes play once per tab session and settle on interaction, reduced motion, print or navigation; a reload (F5) or history visit never replays any of them, read from the navigation type so it holds where storage is blocked, and a return visit never replays a scene already seen (`seen.ts`). Each homepage section settles in that way as the reader reaches it (owner, 29 Oct 2026: “content should appear little by little while scrolling”; `reveal-v2.js`): heading, lead, main block, then foot, armed only below the fold and never on an arrival at a `#section`; a section's own scene starts once its lead has landed (the answer ribbon, How it works' “paid” and then its scene, the roadmap track), and a loop starts once its card has landed, so a reveal and a loop never start together. The announcement bar drops in 3 s into a fresh visit, once the hero has landed (owner, 29 Oct 2026): bar and page wait lifted by the bar's height and slide down together as translates; a scroll, press or key first settles it in place by scrolling the same height, so nothing moves under the reader (`announcement-state.js`, `AnnouncementBar.astro`). Two exceptions play on every load, a reload included (owner, 6 Oct 2026): the hero's “decentralized” underline, which sweeps in and out and answers hover, and the answer-band ribbon, which sweeps once its lead has landed. How it works plays a step's scene every time that step is picked, a reload included, because a pick is the reader asking for it; only its entrance waits for a fresh visit (owner, 26 Oct 2026). Nothing moves through the steps unless a step is picked: a walk down them, built on 29 Oct 2026, read in cold reads as a stepper that had lost its content, so it was cut. Its steps are picked by press or key, never hover. What can Augur Lituus power's cards loop instead (owner, 28 Oct 2026: “all animations should be going in a loop”): one choreography on the 12 s cycle that opens and closes on the finished card, with one part lit at a time (`useCaseTimeline.loop`). It runs while the stage is on screen, a reload included; a pick starts the picked case from the top, pressing the case already shown restarts it, and a press, focus or key in the card stops it on the finished card until the next pick. No entrance animation plays on a reload; loops simply run from their resting frame. Hero arrival settles when the page is hidden; ongoing and timed scenes pause when hidden or off-screen and resume only while visible. No-JavaScript, reduced motion and print render final states. Measure any new ongoing motion. Never hide content behind animation. One exception to the motion rules (owner, 22 Oct 2026): from 1024 px, the hero draws a live canvas floor (`hero-floor-v2.js`), still first, answering fine pointers with a light and clicks or taps with a report; the generated video it replaced stays one switch away in `Hero.astro`. Both are outside the Figma choreography.

Use one tabs controller with keyboard arrows, Home/End, activation mode and labelled panels. Native disclosure buttons and exclusive named details must work by keyboard; no ARIA menu navigation. Native mobile dialog must manage focus. Forms have labels, autocomplete, associated errors and a polite summary. Email composition is not delivery. The newsletter keeps its designed field as a local-only prototype until a provider and consent are approved: it validates in the browser, never sends data and never claims a subscription.
